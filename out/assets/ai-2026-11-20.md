# ai asset: freshness teardown, Lyst and its retail partner Footshop
5 of 5 Footshop pages returned no product content to a plain request. Lyst's own listing page returned content.
3 answered HTTP 406, 2 served a challenge page (HTTP 400). The probe recorded status and size only; nothing was collected.

2026-11-20. Documented gap, not collected data.

## Finding
Lyst's 14 November 2026 Trustpilot reply says an order "couldn't be placed with Footshop because the item is out of stock". The truth Lyst needed sat on Footshop's own pages. A plain, polite request to five Footshop pages returned no product content at all: three answered HTTP 406 and two served a challenge page. Lyst's own listing page returned content. The page that shows the stale answer is reachable; the page that holds the true answer is not, to a naive collector.

## Path
PATH B. No ScrapeGuys API key exists in this repo, so nothing was collected. scripts/probe.py made one unauthenticated request per URL, checked robots.txt first, and stored status and timing only.

## The five facts, and the target list
For any Footshop item Lyst lists: current price, in stock or not, sizes available, sale price, and whether the page still exists. No product is named in any of the three complaints, so these are Footshop's home and stock listing pages, not the specific item that was sold out.

## What this shows about collecting it in house
Footshop refuses a plain client on the first request, every time, in under 700 ms: a 406 on its storefront and a challenge page on its listings. Seeing whether a Footshop item is still in stock therefore takes a collector that passes those defences, run on a schedule, for every retailer of the 27,000 Lyst lists that defends itself this way. How many of those retailers defend themselves like Footshop is UNVERIFIED: this probe measured one. No retries, proxies or browsers were tried, by design, so this measures where a naive attempt fails, not whether a hardened one would succeed.
